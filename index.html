New here? Start with us

Never run with a group before? Perfect — most of us started exactly there. Here's everything you need for your first Overland run.

Your first run

  • Just turn up. No sign-up, no fee. Find us at the meeting point — look for the roundel.
  • All paces welcome. We run in loose pace groups and no one gets left behind.
  • We regroup at every station. A quick breather and a group photo under the roundel before the next leg.
  • Join or peel off anywhere. Every route runs past stations — use the planner above to pick your stretch.
  • Stick around. We usually finish with coffee (and often a pub).

What to bring & staying safe

  • Comfortable trainers and clothes for the weather
  • Water, plus a snack for the longer runs
  • Contactless or Oyster for the ride home
  • Phone — for photos, maps and safety
  • A head-torch or lights for evening canal and night runs
  • A layer to throw on at regroup stops

We share the exact route in advance, stick to lit and populated streets and paths, and keep the group together. Run within your limits, and tell a group lead if you're heading off early.

Route ideas

A library of group-friendly London routes — parks, rivers, canals and landmark tours — to adapt into upcoming runs. Tap a route to trace it on the map (⌘/Ctrl + scroll to zoom), or filter by type and distance.

Run a bus route

Every London bus route — all 676 of them, both directions — as a run. Pick a route to see its stops, distance and rough run/walk time traced on the map, then hit Reverse to run it the other way. Live from the TfL network.

The Superloop

London’s orbital express-bus network — 12 routes (SL1–SL11 and the BL1 Bakerloop) ringing the outer boroughs. Tap any route to trace its stops end to end in its own colour. See the whole loop on the interactive map (Superloop tab), with TfL’s official diagram beside it.

The London LOOP

The London Outer Orbital Path — a ~150-mile (240 km) signed walking route right around the edge of London, in 24 sections between the outer-suburb stations. Grab a GPX for any section for your watch, or see the whole orbital on the interactive map (London LOOP tab). Route data from OpenStreetMap.

The Capital Ring

The London LOOP’s inner sibling — a ~78-mile (125 km) signed walking orbital through inner London’s parks, rivers and backstreets, in 15 sections between the stations. Grab a GPX for any section for your watch, or see the whole ring on the interactive map (Capital Ring tab). Route data from OpenStreetMap.

The line collector

Direction counts — running a line each way is two different runs. Collect both to truly tick it off. The goal? The whole network, both ways, one Sunday at a time.

About Overland

It started with a simple question: what does the Underground actually look like from up top? Overland answers it for the whole network — every line traced above ground, station to station, on routes that hug the tunnels and tracks as closely as the streets allow.

Run it, walk it or ride it: pick a line, see the distance, the elevation and the route, then follow it end to end past every station. It's part run, part exploration of the city most Londoners only ever see from a platform.

  • When Whenever you like — it's self-guided, not a fixture
  • Distance From a 3 km branch to a 30 km main line, split into station legs
  • Pace Yours — walk it, jog it or race it
  • Cost Free. No fare, no sign-up.
